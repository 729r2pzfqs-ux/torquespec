Common Questions

What torque should I use if there is no published spec?

Fall back on the size-and-grade value for the fastener. Identify the diameter and the grade marking, then use the metric or imperial chart on this site — those figures come from ISO 898-1 and SAE J429 with clamp load at 75% of proof load. Treat them as a starting point, not as an override for a manufacturer figure.

Why do component torque specs differ from bolt-size charts?

A bolt-size chart assumes a steel bolt clamping steel. Real assemblies often clamp aluminium, plastic or a gasket, where the joint yields long before the bolt does. That is why a valve cover bolt takes 7 ft·lbs even though an M6 class 8.8 bolt could take twice that.

Are torque specs in dry or lubricated condition?

Unless a manual says otherwise, assume dry, clean threads. Oil or anti-seize cuts friction so the same wrench setting produces roughly 20–30% more clamp load — enough to strip aluminium threads or yield a fastener.
