M3 torque in practice

For a typical M3 class 8.8 bolt the dry figure is 1.31 N·m (0.97 ft·lbs). If the threads are oiled, drop to 0.98 N·m — using the dry number on an oiled bolt would push it about 34% past the intended clamp load. Class 10.9 takes 1.88 N·m, 44% more than 8.8, and the fine M3×0.35 thread raises the 8.8 figure to 1.46 N·m because its stress area is 12% larger.

Small sizes like M3 are mostly machine screws in electronics, sheet-metal assemblies, covers and light brackets. The torque figures are low enough that a click-type 3/8" wrench is at the bottom of its range or below it — use a 1/4" drive wrench or a torque screwdriver, and read the in·lbs column on the detail pages. Stripping the female thread is the usual failure at this size, especially in aluminium or plastic bosses, so the property-class figure is an upper limit when the mating part is weaker than the bolt.
