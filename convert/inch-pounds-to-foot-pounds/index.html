Inch Pounds to Foot Pounds

ft·lbs = in·lbs ÷ 12 · in·lbs = ft·lbs × 12

The Formula

ft·lbs = in·lbs ÷ 12
in·lbs = ft·lbs × 12

Torque is force times distance, so changing the distance unit from feet to inches multiplies the number by twelve while the physical torque stays identical. 240 in·lbs and 20 ft·lbs are the same tightness — one is just written on a finer scale.

Which unit will your spec be in? As a rule of thumb, anything under about 15 ft·lbs is published in inch-pounds: valve cover bolts, oil pan bolts on small engines, intake manifold fasteners, interior trim, electrical terminals and most plastic-threaded fasteners.

Watch the wrench range. Click wrenches are accurate over roughly the top 80% of their scale. A 1/2-inch-drive 20–150 ft·lbs wrench set to 8 ft·lbs is guesswork; use a 1/4-inch or 3/8-inch-drive inch-pound wrench instead.

Need newton-metres too? 1 in·lb = 0.1129848 N·m — see the in·lbs to N·m converter.

Common Questions

How do you convert inch pounds to foot pounds?

Divide by 12. Formula: ft·lbs = in·lbs ÷ 12. Examples: 100 in·lbs = 8.33 ft·lbs; 240 in·lbs = 20 ft·lbs; 600 in·lbs = 50 ft·lbs.

How many inch pounds are in a foot pound?

Exactly 12. A foot-pound is a pound-force applied one foot from the axis; an inch-pound is the same force one inch out, and there are twelve inches in a foot.

What is 100 inch pounds in foot pounds?

100 in·lbs = 8.33 ft·lbs. That is a light spec — typical of valve cover bolts, plastic trim fasteners and small accessory brackets.

Why are some torque specs given in inch pounds?

Because foot-pound figures below about 15 are hard to set accurately on a common 1/2-inch-drive wrench. Manufacturers publish small fasteners in inch-pounds so a 1/4-inch or 3/8-inch drive wrench can be used at a readable point on its scale.

Can you use a foot-pound wrench for an inch-pound spec?

Only if the spec falls inside the wrench's accurate range — generally the top 80% of its scale. A 20–150 ft·lbs wrench cannot reliably set 8 ft·lbs (100 in·lbs). Use an inch-pound wrench for anything under roughly 15 ft·lbs.
